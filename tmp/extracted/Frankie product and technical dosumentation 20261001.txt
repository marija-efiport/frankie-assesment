Frankie
Welcome to the document repository where you can find all you need to know about Frankie.
Content is divided in a way that each person can easily get an overview but also go in depth if
required. 
The pages in front of you will guide you to the right documentation, no matter if you are a
product persona interested in Frankies capabilities and a product overview, a technical persona
who wants to know more about how Frankie systems are operating, a persona in charge of
event organization and/or management who needs to understand the best practices in event
creation and management, a persona managing the conversations or a security expert that
wants to understand the security aspects of Frankie.
Frankie is a collaborative initiative of the Frankfurt School based on the Mindsmiths Platform. It
is an AI solution intended for Frankfurt School students, employees, alumni, and others. Frankie
brings the functionality of event creation and management for the FS staff and a personalized
assistant that connects and facilitates knowledge sharing between Frankfurt School students,
employees, alumni, and other people tied to the Frankfurt School.

Microservices Architecture
The Mindsmiths Platform employs a microservices architecture where each service is
developed and deployed independently. This architectural style enables scalability, fault
isolation, and continuous delivery/deployment. Services within the Mindsmiths Platform are
predominantly written in Python or Java, chosen for their versatility, robustness, and extensive
libraries that support various functionalities.
These microservices simulate the work of autonomous agents. Autonomous agents are self-
operating software entities that perform tasks on behalf of users or other systems with minimal
or no human intervention. They are characterized by their ability to operate independently, adapt
to changing conditions, learn from experiences, and interact with other agents or systems,
making them essential components in complex, dynamic environments such as multi-agent
systems, robotics, and artificial intelligence applications. 
Core Agents 
Relation Manager: Every user has their own relation manager agent. It is in charge of
managing all communication with that user.
Event Manager: In charge of orchestrating everything regarding Frankie's event organization
- from invites, venues, and reminders, to attendance verification, feedback collection, and
others. Provides context and data for communication about events.
ExternalEvent Manager: In charge of managing external Frankie events - invites, venues,
and reminders, and others.
Canteen Manager: Manages proactive notifications about school canteen menus, scrapes
canteen menus, and answers usersʼ questions about the Frankie canteen menu.

Onboarding and Verification
Frankie is capable of onboarding and verifying students and employees. This involves collecting
necessary information, verifying identity through predefined protocols, and granting appropriate
access based on user roles.
User Roles 
The verification process is based on email access: 
For “students”, they are verified via @fs-students.de emails 
For “alumni”, they are verified via emails 
For “employees”, they are verified via @fs.de emails 
Everyone else is categorized as “Other” 
Students who donʼt have access to their @fs-students.de address, as well as employees who
donʼt have access to their @fs.de address, are classified as “Other”. 
Duplicate Accounts
In case the same email address is used twice (for 2 different phone numbers), the older user will
be disabled, and the new phone number will be associated with that email address. This was
done in order to best handle users changing their phone numbers.
Email Verification 
Connecting a user with an email address is done using verification links sent via submitted email
address: after asking users for their email, a verification link is auto-generated. Once the user
opens the verification email and clicks on the button redirecting to the verification link, they are
connected to that email address. By connecting, they receive all the attributes associated with
that email (for current students this means their program name, intake year, student advisor
information etc.) 
Rich Context and Functions 
Verified emails are used to give more context about users, since those emails have different
attributes in the FS systems. At the moment, these are the additional information we add to the
onboarded current students:
Matrikelnummer
First name (entered during onboarding flow)

Event Organization
Frankieʼs Event Management solution is designed to help the Frankfurt School engage students
more effectively throughout the entire event lifecycle. By integrating WhatsApp, their preferred
communication channel, you can keep students in the loop, build stronger relationships through
regular, meaningful contact, and tailor invitations based on their interests. The solution also
enables you to collect feedback, track early signs of interest to better forecast attendance,
reduce no-shows, and stay connected after events to foster long-term engagement.
The purpose of this document is to provide an overview of the Event Management
functionalities in Frankie. As one of the platform's core features, Event Management enables FS
stakeholders to easily organize, monitor, and manage all types of events, allowing them to
create and edit the entire event journey seamlessly through the Dashboard.
1. Terms and Definitions
Dashboard - Serves as the control center for managing the entire event lifecycle. It enables
organizers to oversee event setup, monitor student engagement, and track progress in real-
time.
Invitation Flow - The student-facing interface for receiving event invitations. It enables
students to quickly express interest, provide initial feedback, or decline, creating a more
interactive and personalized experience.
Ticketing System - Includes a QR code generator for students upon registration and a QR
scanner for organizers to verify attendance at the event. This system streamlines check-ins
and ensures accurate attendance tracking.
Student Feedback Flow - Post-event screens presented to students whose attendance had
been confirmed. It allows them to leave comments and provide an NPS (Net Promoter Score),
helping organizers collect valuable insights.
Final Report - Automatically generated after the event concludes. It includes comprehensive
data such as total registrations, attendance numbers, and feedback summaries, providing a
full picture of event performance.
Interim Report - Available on the Dashboard before the event takes place. It shows real-time
application data to help organizers gauge interest and adjust their preparations accordingly.
Organizer Questions - A set of prompts that the event organizer must complete to inform
content creation and ensure the event is aligned with student needs and expectations.
Frankie assists users in organizing events by:

2. Event Flow & Elements (Student View)
The student-facing experience is built entirely within Armory (explained in technical
documentation), with all communication delivered through WhatsApp, ensuring seamless,
real-time interaction on a platform students actively use. The experience follows a structured
flow:
1. Event Invitation
Students receive an invitation via WhatsApp and can respond in one of three ways:
a. Interested – They proceed to register and receive further event details.
b. Not interested, but want reminders – They opt out of this event but stay subscribed for
future invitations.
c. Not interested, no reminders – They opt out of both this event and future notifications.
2. Attendance Confirmation
A confirmation message is sent to students who registered, helping solidify attendance and
enabling better tracking.
3. Second Invitation
A follow-up invite is sent to students who didnʼt respond to the initial message, giving them
another chance to engage.
4. Reminder
Sent 24/48 hours before the event to all confirmed attendees, helping reduce no-shows and
maintain awareness.
5. Additional Screens
Optional screens may appear depending on event configuration, such as clicking on the
invitation for the event when the event has passed, is full, etc.

3. Event Flow & Elements (Organizator View)
1. QR Code Scanner
During the event, organizers use the built-in QR code scanner in Armory to check students in.
Each student presents their unique QR code (received via WhatsApp), and the scanner verifies
attendance instantly.
Organizers receive access to the scanner via email within the time window defined during event
setup in the Dashboard. There is no need to install any application—organizers can simply click
the button provided in the email to launch the scanner directly in their mobile browser.
Each scanner link is unique and cannot be shared between users. If multiple people need
access to the scanner (e.g., additional staff or volunteers), the organizer can trigger additional
scanner links by using the same email button and entering the email address of the person who
needs access. A separate scanner will then be sent to that individual.
In addition to verifying student attendance, the QR code scanner also allows organizers to issue
tickets on the spot for students who arrive without one, offering greater flexibility and ensuring
no student is turned away unnecessarily.
2. Event Organization through Dashboard
An event is organized through the Dashboard in a few simple steps:
1. Creating a new event
2. Entering general event information
3. Setting dates and times related to invitations and applications
4. Adding venues and seats
5. Editing event content
6. Reviewing and publishing the event
7. Monitoring events
Once the event is published and invitations are sent, you can begin monitoring engagement and
participation through the Dashboard.

Kowledgebase Overview
BookStack Integration
To manage the growing number of documents detailing expert knowledge required by Frankie
BookStack was introduced. BookStack is a simple, self-hosted, easy-to-use platform for
organising and storing information. The information on Frankieʼs BookStack is organized in the
“shelves” containing the “books”, each containing one or more paragraphs gathered for the
knowledge base.
Shelves are separated by the languages to make Frankieʼs conversation more natural in each
language
Why have we implemented bookstack as centralized knowledgebase
Frankie is an AI assistant used by students (and other user groups in the future) at Frankfurt
School of Finance and Management to answer school-related questions via WhatsApp. Frankie
19

--- PAGE 20 ---
is based on a popular tool, ChatGPT, with access to locally stored information (“knowledge
base”). 
In order for the service to work as expected and answer questions related to Frankfurt School,
the assistant pulls the information from the manually curated local knowledge base. The local
knowledge base for Frankie is currently accessible only through the Frankie platform (effectively
GUI control panel for Frankie), together with the rest of the controls for the assistant. 
The local knowledge base is stored in YAML format files, from which Frankie pulls the
information related to the prompt (message from the end user of the service) sent through
WhatsApp. 
BookStack is a well-known and maintained open-source wiki platform for organizing and storing
information. It was found as a solution that satisfies all of our needs and requirements. The idea
is to introduce BookStack as a front-end editor for the knowledge base. Disclaimer: Technically
speaking, BookStack will also have its own database and store information, which will then be
transferred via API to the Frankie platform and converted into YAML format. 
Access control - Frankie platform is built functionally to support control of the whole assistant
and therefore does not enable the end user to access only certain controls such as the
knowledge base. However, the actual knowledge base should always reside and be accessible
through the Frankie platform as it is the endpoint from which Frankie pulls the information from.
BookStack allows us to safely segment the knowledge base from other critical system controls,
reducing the risk of accidental service disruptions that could occur if non-technical users were
granted access to the Frankie platform. 
BookStack also enables us to provide access to the knowledge base to other potential
knowledge base maintainers (FS Staff members). Out of the box, BookStack also comes with a
user permission system that will allow us to segment parts of the knowledge base as well—so
that only authorized users can access and modify certain parts. 
Search - Study-related changes (all of them need to end up in the knowledge base) happen
constantly, and even now, the knowledge base consists of hundreds of pages, which will
continue to grow in the future. BookStack comes with a global search capability, which enables
knowledge base maintainers to keep track of existing information with ease. 
Version history and transparency - BookStack comes with a system for tracking file changes.
This transparency will help ensure the accuracy of the knowledge base over time and make it
easier to identify and reverse incorrect or unwanted changes. This is currently not possible as
we can only roll back to the previous version of the knowledge base file on the Frankie platform. 
20

--- PAGE 21 ---
WYSIWYG text editor - Simplicity in editing is the core principle of BookStack. The WYSIWYG
editor will make it easy for non-technical users to contribute, requiring little to no onboarding.
Meanwhile, YAML styling (end result of the knowledge base file) has a learning curve and zero
tolerance for styling errors. 
In conclusion, BookStack allow us to streamline the management of the knowledge base by
providing robust access controls, improved searchability, version tracking, and an intuitive
interface for non-technical contributors. Introducing BookStack will ensure that Frankie
continues to deliver accurate and up-to-date information, while minimizing the risk to the
serviceʼs stability.

Lunch Menu
Frankie proactively notifies subscribed users about the daily and weekly menus in the school
canteen. This feature helps users plan their meals and stay informed about the available food
options. 
Lunch Menu Subscription 
Not long after finishing the initial onboarding process, users are prompted with an offer to
subscribe to the daily or weekly lunch menu. 
Opting for the daily lunch menu subscription means Frankie will be sending the daily lunch menu
early in the morning for every day the daily menu is defined (e.g. not on Sundays, because the
canteen is closed). Alternatively, subscribing to the weekly lunch menu means Frankie will send
the link to the website listing the canteenʼs weekly menu every Monday morning. 
Frankie keeps track of how often subscribed users show interest in the lunch menu messages.
Not reacting to the messages sent as part of the lunch menu subscription for long enough will
signal Frankie to prompt the user with an offer to cancel the active subscription. From there,
users can choose to switch to another type of subscription or cancel the subscription
completely. 
If at some point the user changes their mind, Frankie will prompt them with the lunch menu
subscription offer when the user starts talking about the lunch menu. 
Intent Recognition 
Regardless of the subscription, users are able to ask Frankie about the menu at any point during
the conversation. Frankie will understand the user's intent and check if there is a menu for the
requested day.
For cases when the menu is available, Franke will provide the user with the list of menu items
along with the prices for each meal. Since users can ask for a menu at any time, on Sundays
and holidays when the canteen is closed users will receive an appropriate message about the
menu not being available for the day.

User Involvement in Canteen Menu Creation

Frankie encourages user participation in creating new canteen menus. It gathers suggestions
and preferences from users, facilitating a collaborative approach to menu planning.
Menu Poll Creation Process
Besides expressing feedback about the lunch options in the canteen, which will be discussed in
more detail in the next section, users can participate in menu creation by voting in one of the
canteen menu polls. The voting feature allows administrators to send WhatsApp messages with
interactive buttons to a specific group of users. Prerequisite for this is to create a WhatsApp
template message which contains text in which poll question is defined (e.g. Vote for the cuisine
youʼd like to see on the menu next week.) and a list of interactive quick reply buttons which
serve as options for the poll created (e.g. Mexican).  
Once they receive a lunch menu poll message, users can pick any option from the list of
displayed message buttons. Any subsequent choice of the other options will overwrite the
previous choice and only the last option picked will be taken into account. They are able to
select their choice within a predefined period set during the creation of the new poll. If a user
votes after the poll is expired, Frankie will send an appropriate message about the period for
poll answering being expired. 
During the time the poll is active, administrators are able to check the current status of a poll,
namely number of votes per option as well as percentages are shown for each poll. Generally,
the dashboard contains every poll previously created and shows, in addition to the information
previously stated, basic information about the poll including the poll question and date of
creation. It is also possible to select a subset of polls and send results as a report to a mail
configurable from the configuration on the dashboard.

Feedback Collection and Categorization
Frankie collects feedback on various aspects such as: 
The Frankie system itself 
School-related matters 
Lunch options in the canteen 
Recent events 
The feedback is categorized to help the relevant teams address issues and improve services.
Feedback Collection Logic 
Feedback collection is done based on the conversation between the user and Frankie on
WhatsApp. Conversation is analyzed after the period of the userʼs inactivity. This period is set to
30 minutes by default and is configurable in the Dashboard. This feedback collection process is
done automatically and repeats for every userʼs inactivity period. 
If user sends a message and/or makes a proactive interaction, Frankie identifies if they provided
constructive feedback in one or more categories:
SCHOOL: feedback regarding the Frankfurt school, how it works, etc.
LUNCH: feedback regarding the canteen, the food, or the menus
ASSISTANT: feedback regarding the assistant, conversations, knowledge, etc.
Group of users can be selected based on their tags (preferences, attributes, etc.) or they can be
selected as individuals.

Sentiment Analysis and Alerts
Frankie automatically analyzes the sentiment in conversations with users. If negative sentiments
are detected, the system alerts the Frankie team, enabling them to promptly address any
concerns or issues raised by users. 
Similar to the feedback collection process, sentiments are analyzed based on the WhatsApp
messages exchanged during one conversation session. The conversation session ends 30
minutes after the last message has been sent. By sending a new message more than 30
minutes after the last one, a new conversation session will be created. Every message
exchanged between user and Frankie during this period will serve as a content based on which
sentiments will be extracted. 
Sentiment Categories 
If user sends a message and/or makes a proactive interaction, Frankie identifies if they showed
one of the following emotion:
POSITIVE: if the user is likely feeling happy, satisfied, pleased, or other
NEUTRAL: if the user is showing no particular emotion
NEGATIVE: if the user is likely feeling frustrated, angry, dissatisfied, or other
In situations when a negative sentiment is detected, the Frankie team gets the tag along with
the messages from the conversation excerpt to provide context for the possible intervention via
UCS or by adding new content to the knowledge base. This mechanism is explained in the
Analyzing Conversation Excerpts section. This enables the Frankie team to analyze the reasons
behind the negative sentiment and jump into the conversation if needed.
NOT DEVELOPED
Overview of Collected Sentiments
Once detected, sentiments are stored and displayed on the Dashboard. To help navigate
through collected sentiments, filters are added to select sentiments based on the userʼs name,
sentimentʼs type and date when sentiment was stored.

Communication Integration
Frankie is integrated with popular communication channels, real-time communication is done via
WhatsApp, notifications/alerts are sent via Email, and the Human-In-The-Loop protocol is
enabled through FS Dashboard. This ensures seamless interaction with users and system
managers. 
WhatsApp Integration
WhatsApp integration is powered by Infobip. The current implementation allows Frankie to:
Read userʼs phone number and WhatsApp name 
Send text messages, images, videos in real time 
Receive text messages, images, videos in real time 
Mark messages as read 
Send buttons and collect button clicks 
Send proactive, templated messages 
Email Integration
Emails are sent by using the Google Gmail API. This allows Frankie to send large-scale emails
without raising suspicion with email providers.

Frankie Platform Dashboard
This section serves as a starting point for monitoring the key performance indicators regarding
the main Frankie functionalities. The landing page of the dashboard is divided into four sections:
Business Growth
User Segmentation
Value Creating moments
Real-Time Analysis.
These sections provide different angles of tracking the user engagement, satisfaction, activity
and all around experience.
First section, Business Growth, calculates and displays metrics regarding the total number of
users and messages exchanged during the conversation. It tracks the number of users active in
the predefined period (e.g. 24 hours, 7 days, 1 month). Active user is the user who sent at least
one message to Frankie in a given period. Out of all messages sent by Frankie, this section
provides insights into how many of them were questions being answered, feedback collected on
various issues and canteen menus shown. Similar stats are provided for the messages sent by
users.
Second section, User Segmentation, serves as an insight into the distribution of users based on
their association with the Frankfurt School, categorizing them into one of the following
categories:
Student: active students currently enrolled in one of the programs at FS
FS Staff: employees at FS
Alumni: ex-students who graduated at FS
Other: users who are not directly connected with the FS
Unregistered: users who did not successfully complete the onboarding process.
Similarly, the number of active students who interact with Frankie are displayed per program
they have enrolled in being categorized in programs such as “Bachelor in Business
Administration”, “Master of Finance” or “Foundation Year”. During the onboarding process, users
are asked to submit their interests such as interest in “Business & Entrepreneurship”, “Sports &

Fitness” or “Arts & Culture” and the distribution of interests is shown as well on the monitoring
dashboard.
Value Creating Moments serve as a guide to how helpful Frankie has been to the students based
on the functionalities supported. This section keeps track of how often has lunch menu been
shown to students on a daily and weekly basis for a given period, how many new subscriptions
have been created, how many events have been organized, invites sent, what was the
application acceptance rate as well as the attendance rate.
Finally, Real-time Analysis serves as a basis for a more detailed inspection of the user
interaction via WhatsApp messages providing day-by-day representation of messages

Frankie Skills (Technical)
Frankie is a modular, intelligent assistant built on the Mindsmiths platform, designed to facilitate
key user-facing processes at Frankfurt School. Each of its capabilities operates as a dedicated
service or agent, communicating over a message-driven architecture. Together, these skills
create a seamless, personalized user experience by orchestrating onboarding, event
management, knowledge access, and conversational interaction.
Onboarding
The OnboardingManager skill manages both proactive and reactive onboarding flows. It
handles:
Email and student ID verification
Preference collection
Terms of service acceptance
CRM data integration and event invitation alignment
This service ensures that every new user is smoothly introduced into the system, with all
required checks and contextual data captured up front. Built on a context-aware, event-driven
architecture, it dynamically adapts to user inputs and can progress users through onboarding
flows automatically.
Event Management
The event skill allows Frankie to support the full lifecycle of event planning and interaction:
Enforces single-organizer ownership per event to reduce complexity
Supports transferring event ownership
Allows organizers to segment audiences based on academic program or semester
Enables personalized event content tailored to user groups
Facilitates structured assistant-led user interaction about events through predefined
conversational flows
This system ensures scalability while maintaining relevance and personalization for each user
group.
Intent Routing
Frankie's core conversational engine is powered by a configurable intent-routing mechanism.
This mechanism maps user messages to structured intents, enabling:
91

--- PAGE 92 ---
Natural language understanding across domains (onboarding, events, lunch, general inquiries,
etc.)
Dynamic dispatch to specific agents or handlers for fulfillment
Schema-based interpretation of user intent and required context
This subsystem enables Frankie to scale across diverse functional areas without compromising
maintainability or user experience.
Knowledge Base Integration
The Knowledge Base skill syncs documentation from BookStack into an internal semantic
index, enabling:
Live retrieval of structured documentation
Intelligent reasoning and contextual responses
Both keyword and semantic search
By treating documentation as an active data source, Frankie can provide high-quality, relevant
answers to user queries based on institutional knowledge.
Lunch Menu
The Lunch Menu skill delivers multilingual, real-time canteen menu updates through WhatsApp:
Scrapes menu data from a third-party provider
Supports proactive notifications (daily/weekly)
Handles on-demand queries via message
Operates on user subscriptions and configurable time triggers
This service ensures that students always have access to the latest meal information in a
conversational, low-effort format.
Feedback Collection and Categorization
This component analyzes natural conversations with Frankie to extract:
Sentiment classification (positive, neutral, negative)
Categorical feedback tagged to the School, Lunch, or Assistant experience
It uses lightweight NLU techniques to identify insights without requiring explicit feedback forms.
The results feed into dashboards and summaries, providing actionable intelligence for
institutional improvement.

Onboarding and Verification (Technical)
Overview
The OnboardingManager service is a key component of the FS system responsible for
initiating, managing, and verifying user onboarding. It handles both proactive and reactive
onboarding flows, including email and student ID verification, preferences setup, terms of
service acceptance, and integration with event invitations and CRM data.
This service is built on the Mindsmiths framework and operates through a message-driven
architecture, using event listeners (via @on_message) and context-based APIs to coordinate
user data and progression.
Key Concepts and Components
Onboarding Flows:
Shadow Onboarding: Used to onboard users implicitly before they actively engage with
the platform.
Active Onboarding: Users are prompted via messages or events to proceed with
onboarding.
Wizards and Screens: Users are guided through onboarding via dynamic screens ("wizards")
tailored to their role (e.g., student, alumni).
Email and Student ID Verification:
Email verification uses pin-based validation with email templates.
Student ID verification cross-references CRM data and validates name matching.
Terms of Service Handling:
Tracks user agreement.
Updates CRM and user context accordingly.
APIs Used:
RelationManagerAPI: Handles user context and interactions.
CrmAPI: Used for student lookup and data validation.
ChronosAPI: Schedules follow-up tasks (e.g., onboarding reminders).
EmailAdapterAPI: Sends verification and invitation emails.
EventsManagementAPI: Sends invitations to onboarding events.
Data Models:
94

--- PAGE 95 ---
OnboardingProcess: Stores temporary onboarding state, such as verification code and
flags.
Integration Overview
The OnboardingManager service integrates with the FS system through:
Messaging Infrastructure: Listens to and emits messages to coordinate onboarding steps
based on user interactions or backend triggers.
Mindsmiths Configuration and Context: Uses the Mindsmiths configuration system to define
settings (e.g., time delays, mappings) and the context system to retrieve user identifiers.
External Systems:
CRM Integration: Validates student data against existing FS student records.
Email Adapter: Delivers structured templates for onboarding prompts.
Event System: Sends invitations as part of the onboarding sequence.
Functional Breakdown
prompt_to_onboard – Sends onboarding invitations via WhatsApp.
prompt_to_join_event – Shadow-onboards the user, schedules an invitation.
shadow_onboard_user – Prepares user context without full verification.
handle_email_submitted – Starts email verification by sending PIN email.
check_verification_code – Validates email via PIN code.
handle_student_id_submitted – Matches student ID and name with CRM.
after_student_lookup – Validates CRM record and updates context.
handle_terms_of_service_submitted – Confirms user accepted ToS.
handle_association_with_fs_submitted – Guides users based on their affiliation.
handle_interests_submitted – Routes user to follow-up screens.
update_preferences – Sends templates to configure personal preferences.
send_event_invite – Sends a configured first message invitation.
Configuration

eventSentAsFirstMessage: Event ID used for initial onboarding invite.
associationWithFsToPersonaMapping: Mapping between user roles and assistant

sendOnboardingInvitationToShadowOnboardedUserAfter: Delay before inviting

Events Organization (Technical)
Events organization
Model implementation breakdown
1. Event content management
This section addresses the following assumptions:
Every event should have only one organizer to reduce complexity and chance for errors,
Ownership of an event should be transferable between organizers in order to provide
flexibility,
Organizer needs to be able to segment attendees based on the semester and program they
are in in order to be relevant for an event,
Organizer needs to be able to define personalized content for different target groups of leads
since the content will depend on groupsʼ preferences,
Assistant will interact with leads about events through a predefined flow in order to reduce
system complexity at this stage.
Event content management is split into two parts:
1. event model that defines the core information about the event in question,
event_content model that defines what texts and images will be displayed for which target
group, with some other supporting functionalities.
97

--- PAGE 98 ---
The event model contains the field status that indicates if the event is in draft, is active, finished,
or canceled. It contains fields title and description that serve as identifying attributes for
organizers looking for particular events.
There are important dates and times which are to be defined during the event creation and
which determine when the important information will be communicated to the leads and
attendees:
starts_at - when the event will take place
ends_at - when the event will end
invite_send_at - from when invitations should be sent to the leads
second_invite_send_at - from when second invitations should be sent to the leads
application_deadline - when applications for physical seats should be closed
reminder_send_at - when event reminder should be sent to attendees that registered
feedback_send_at - when event feedback should be sent to attendees that registered
qr_scannder_send_at - when QR code scanner should be sent to organizer
The event_content model contains a foreign key reference to an event. This allows for multiple 
event_content objects per event object. This is important because event_content stores content
for a particular target group. Personalized content that Frankie displays to the attendees is
stored in this model, and it can be different for every target group. Content that is currently
98

--- PAGE 99 ---
identified as customizable: WhatsApp description, intro image and text, and additional screen
that describes the reason this event is recommended to the attendee. In case when attendees
fall into multiple target groups, the target_group_priority defines event_content that will be used.
The target_group allows for segmentation by tags assigned to the leads (e.g. program names
and intake years). The logical operator between multiple tags is or.
Changing organizers on events will currently be supported by contacting the administrator who
will make the appropriate changes.
2. Event Venue
This section addresses the following assumptions:
Assistant should support 3 ways of holding events - in-person, virtual and hybrid since thatʼs
FSFMʼs practice,
Assistant wonʼt provide support for limiting venue access to specific target groups.
The event model keeps the venue type - physical, virtual, or hybrid. Attributes for each of those
venue types are stored in their own table. 
The physical_venue holds information about the physical location. Through fields address, room
and details, the organizer should be able define exactly where the event is physically located,
and any additional information about how to get there. Field available_spotsdefines the capacity
of the venue.
The virtual_venue holds information about the virtual location. The url field holds the URL for the
attendees to join online.

Conversational Skills (Technical)
Overview
The systemʼs conversational abilities are structured around a configurable intent-routing
mechanism that enables dynamic handling of user messages across domains such as
onboarding, events, lunch menus, general chat, and more. The goal of this subsystem is to
understand user messages in natural language and map them to actionable intents using well-
defined schemas. Each intent is tied to a specific agent or handler which performs the
corresponding action.
Core Concepts
Intents: Declarative definitions representing user goals or message categories. Each intent
includes:
A name (e.g., ask_about_upcoming_events)
A description explaining when it should be used
An assignee indicating which handler will respond
A parameters schema defining expected inputs (in JSON Schema format)
A list of annotated examples for training or rule-matching
Routing Rules: User messages are matched against intent examples and evaluated using a
cascade of conditional logic (e.g., onboarding status, message content, message history) to
determine the correct intent to invoke.
Assignees: Logical targets for each intent. These include internal modules such as
onboarding_manager, canteen_manager, events_handler, or fallbacks like
faq/frankie_for_unregistered_users.
Parameter Extraction: Intents use a lightweight schema to extract structured data from user
queries (e.g., event name, language, menu date). This supports both functional execution

(e.g., send a menu) and downstream logging/analytics.
Templates and Actions: Certain intents (e.g., frankie_menu_upper_case) return preconfigured
WhatsApp message templates with buttons, text, and follow-up interaction paths.

Feedback Collection and Sentiment Analysis (Technical)
Overview
The Feedback Collection and Categorization component is a specialized system module
designed to extract actionable insights from user interactions with the Assistant. It classifies
user sentiment and identifies thematic feedback related to the Frankfurt School, the 
canteen/lunch service, or the Assistant itself.
The goal is to surface qualitative user insights that can be used to improve institutional services,
enhance the Assistant's performance, and provide decision-makers with a structured overview
of student satisfaction and pain points.
This system does not depend on explicit feedback forms. Instead, it analyzes natural
conversations with the Assistant and applies automated tagging and sentiment classification
using lightweight natural language understanding (NLU) techniques.
Functional Responsibilities
The system is responsible for:
Detecting user sentiment during a conversation (e.g., frustration, satisfaction, neutrality).
Identifying constructive feedback themes based on a predefined schema.
Summarizing each conversation into a compact, human-readable format.
Outputting structured data that can be used for analytics, dashboards, or manual review.
Data Schema
The output of each analyzed conversation includes two main fields under the extractedData
structure:
sentiment
A string value identifying the user's emotional state:
125

--- PAGE 126 ---
POSITIVE: The user expresses happiness, gratitude, or satisfaction.
NEUTRAL: No clear emotional expression is detected.
NEGATIVE: The user expresses frustration, disappointment, or dissatisfaction.
Default: Empty string (if no emotion is expressed).
feedback
A list of tagged feedback categories, identifying the topic of the user's complaint, suggestion, or
praise:
SCHOOL: Feedback about infrastructure, facilities, processes, or general FS matters.
LUNCH: Feedback related to food quality, menu variety, cafeteria hours, or pricing.
ASSISTANT: Feedback on Assistant accuracy, responsiveness, tone, or general usefulness.
Default: Empty string (if no feedback is detected).
Integration and Operation
The system is typically triggered post-conversation and operates as a background analysis
task.
1. The conversation transcript (user and Assistant messages) is passed into the analysis
prompt.
2. The summary field captures a two-to-three sentence explanation of the interaction.
3. The extractedData structure is populated with sentiment and feedback tags, if any are
present.
4. Outputs can be forwarded to CRM trackers, visualization dashboards, or feedback
aggregation systems.

lightweight but meaningful classification. Prompts are optimized for reliability and aligned with

5.1 BookStack Manager
5.1.1 Overview
BookStack is an open-source platform designed for creating and organizing documentation in a structured, user-friendly
manner. It provides a hierarchical content model built around Books, Chapters, and Pages, allowing for easy navigation and
versioned knowledge management.
In Mindsmiths platform, the BookStack Manager serves as the central logic for retrieving file contents from the BookStack as
well as the list of tags assigned to it. The integration supports content, tags and other metadata (e.g. date of file creation and
the last file update) retrieval through the BookStack API, allowing dynamic updates and contextual linking to enable defining
different access to the file content based on the type of users.
This integration is primarily inbound, with our system querying BookStack for page data, metadata, and structure. However, it
also supports outbound updates for checking the length of the paragraphs created on the BookStack pages to make sure that
the paragraphs do not exceed the maximum length. 
Typical use cases include:
Fetching newly created files from the BookStack, parsing the paragraphs, creating
embedding vectors for the paragraphs and storing them in the knowledge base.
Fetching latest updated files from the BookStack, parsing the paragraphs, creating
embedding vectors for the paragraphs and overriding existing ones for these files in the
knowledge base.
Detecting outdated/deleted files from the knowledge base which exist no more on the
BookStack pages and removing them from the knowledge base.
This integration is internal-only, authenticated via service tokens, and operates under clearly scoped permissions to protect
content integrity and maintain role-based access control.

5.1.2 Synchronization logic 
The BookStack Manager is a background service that facilitates the synchronization of
documentation content between the BookStack system and the internal knowledge base. Its
purpose is to ensure that knowledge stored in BookStack—such as manuals, guides, and
instructional content for the students, employees and alumni —is kept up to date, accessible,
and properly structured within the broader platform ecosystem.
The integration supports:
Detecting and processing new, updated, or deleted pages in BookStack.
Converting content into a structured, paragraph-level format.
Validating content and managing access control based on tags.
Automatically updating the knowledge base on a scheduled interval.
When started, the service receives and applies integration-specific settings, including:
The base URL and credentials for BookStack.
Update frequency, expressed as a time interval.
Limits on paragraph length.
Rules governing which content should be publicly available or restricted, based on tagging.
The service initializes its connection to the BookStack API using these configuration parameters
and immediately begins monitoring the documentation system. The service performs
continuous background checks for documentation changes. Each cycle includes three core
operations:
1. Removing Deleted Pages
The system compares the current list of knowledge base files with the active pages available
in BookStack. If a page has been deleted from BookStack, its corresponding entry is removed
from the internal knowledge base. Additionally, pages that lack tags may also be removed,
depending on configuration.

2. Adding New Pages
Newly created pages in BookStack that have not yet been added to the knowledge base are

and storage workflow.
3. Updating Modified Pages
Pages that have been recently updated (within a configurable time window) are fetched and
reprocessed to ensure the most current version is reflected in the knowledge base.
Once a page is identified for creation or update:
The service retrieves the full page content and any associated metadata, including tags.
The content is split into individual paragraphs, omitting the page title and empty lines.
Each paragraph is evaluated against a token-length limit to avoid storing excessively long text.
Valid paragraphs are formatted into a structured data format suitable for the knowledge base.
Paragraphs are then stored, with each entry uniquely identified and linked back to the original
page.
Tagging within BookStack is used to determine visibility rules:
Pages that include a pre-defined "public" tag are marked as accessible to all users.
Pages with other tags are stored with access restrictions, limiting availability to specific
groups or roles based on the tag names.
This ensures sensitive or internal content is not inadvertently exposed.
The update cycle runs continuously at a configurable interval. After each cycle, the service
pauses for the defined duration before starting again. This scheduling allows for near-real-time
synchronization without placing excessive load on either system.
The integration includes extensive logging to aid in debugging, monitoring, and auditing:
Key events like file creation, update, or deletion are logged with identifiers.
Errors during synchronization are captured and logged, but do not stop the service from
continuing its operation.

BookStack API, for retrieving documentation content and metadata.

The HTTP Adapter is a service component built on the Mindsmiths platform that enables
flexible, authenticated HTTP communication with external services. It plays a central role in
handling HTTP requests to the Mindsmiths platform, especially during the interaction with the
Armory (e.g. the onboarding process uses this service to handle confirmation link requests).
This adapter is event-driven, configuration-managed, and built with asynchronous I/O to handle
scalable, robust HTTP operations, including support for both Bearer Token and Basic
Authentication. The service is designed to run persistently and expose an internal AIOHTTP-
based server for receiving HTTP requests when configured to do so.
5.4.2 Key Capabilities
Asynchronous HTTP communication using aiohttp
Support for multiple authentication strategies:
Bearer Token (with optional token refresh)
Basic Authentication
Dynamic configuration management through Mindsmiths dashboard
Auto-start and shutdown logic for embedded HTTP server (receiver)
Robust error handling and token storage using Mindsmiths Store
Configurable runtime behavior via Mindsmiths configs (e.g., toggle HTTP receiver on/off)
5.4.3 Lifecycle and Configuration
Initialization
The adapter is initialized with forge.setup('http_adapter').
Upon start, it sends a ConfigValidationSchema to the Mindsmiths dashboard to validate
configuration fields defined in the Configuration schema.
Config Binding
When new configurations are received via on_config, the service:
Loads http_adapter config block
Conditionally starts or stops the internal HTTP receiver depending on 
receivingEnabled
HTTP Server (Receiver)
Controlled via the receivingEnabled and servicePort settings.
Internally served via uvicorn.Server on a dedicated thread.
Handles HTTP requests (defined in server.py), useful for testing or receiving inbound
calls.

5.4.4 Authenticated Request Handling
The adapter listens for request events, which support:
method: HTTP verb (e.g., GET, POST)
url: Destination URL
Optional payload (data or json)
Optional headers, params
Optional auth: Either BasicAuth or BearerAuth
The request dispatcher determines auth type and handles accordingly:
5.4.5 Bearer Token Authentication
1. Checks if a valid token exists in the Mindsmiths Store.
2. If not, it calls _authorize() to obtain one.
3. Attach the token to the header.
4. If the request fails with 401, a token refresh via _refresh_token() is attempted.
5.4.6 Basic Authentication
Constructs an aiohttp.BasicAuth object using provided credentials.
Sends a request with basic auth attached.
5.4.7 Token Management
Tokens are stored with unique keys based on the authorization URL:
token-<auth.authorizationUrl>
refresh-token-<auth.authorizationUrl>
5.4.8 Error Handling
All request flows are wrapped in try/except blocks. On failure, the adapter returns:
HttpErrorReply including:
HTTP status code (or 1 for unknown exceptions)
Error message and content (e.g., 401 Unauthorized, Timeout, ConnectionError)
For recoverable token issues (e.g., expired tokens), the adapter attempts automatic retries after
refreshing credentials.
5.4.9 Extensibility & Customization
Authorization schema can be extended to support OAuth2 or custom API key-based auth.
Request parameters (headers, params, json) allow flexible payload formatting.

Conversation Lifecycle
Whenever a user (student, alumni, FS staff member, or others) sends a message to Frankie on
WhatsApp, Frankie will reply with single or multiple messages. Such exchange of messages is
considered a conversation. 
Conversation excerpt
Conversation excerpt is a snapshot of one or more conversations that the user had with Frankie.
Snapshots are created once a defined time-window* passes and the user doesn't send any new
messages. For example, if it has been more than 30 minutes since the last message from a user,
a conversation excerpt will be created. Once a conversation excerpt is created, it will become
available for validation on Frankie dashboard under Validate Conversation tab.
*Time-window can be customized. Currently it is 30 minutes.
Conversation excerpts are not considered tickets. Only if a Conversation validator finds an issue
in the conversation excerpt, a ticket can be created to track resolution and any necessary
updates to the knowledge base.

Creating a New Event
Every event creation process starts with a brief set of questions that help define the structure
and content of the event. These questions ensure the event is tailored to its audience and
properly set up in the system.
Questions for Organizers:
1. What is the title of the event?
2. How would you describe the event in a few sentences?
3. Who is your desired audience?
4. What is the date and time of the event?
5. How long will it last?
6. Is it on-site, virtual, or hybrid?
7. If itʼs on-site, whatʼs the location and how many seats are available?
8. Whatʼs the application deadline?
9. Who is the contact person for the event?
The event editing process is initiated by clicking the “+ New Event” button. From there, follow
these steps:
1. Enter General Event Information
Begin by entering the Event Title and Display Name. The Display Name is what students will
see in WhatsApp communications.
2. Select the Organizer
Choose an organizer from the dropdown menu or create a new one directly within the form if
needed.
3. Add Event Description
Provide a short description of the event. This helps Frankie understand the context and refer
to the event appropriately in messages.
4. Enable Ticketing (Optional)
Toggle the Ticketing option if you want QR code tickets to be automatically sent to students
once they accept the invitation.

Event Flow & Elements - Student View
The student-facing experience is built entirely within Armory (explained in technical
documentation), with all communication delivered through WhatsApp, ensuring seamless,
real-time interaction on a platform students actively use. The experience follows a structured
flow:
1. Event Invitation
Students receive an invitation via WhatsApp and can respond in one of three ways:
a. Interested – They proceed to register and receive further event details.
b. Not interested, but want reminders – They opt out of this event but stay subscribed for
future invitations.
c. Not interested, no reminders – They opt out of both this event and future notifications.
2. Attendance Confirmation
A confirmation message is sent to students who registered, helping solidify attendance and
enabling better tracking.
3. Second Invitation
A follow-up invite is sent to students who didnʼt respond to the initial message, giving them
another chance to engage.
4. Reminder
Sent 24/48 hours before the event to all confirmed attendees, helping reduce no-shows and
maintain awareness.
5. Additional Screens
Optional screens may appear depending on event configuration, such as clicking on the
invitation for the event when the event has passed, is full, etc.
6. Feedback
After the event, students are prompted via WhatsApp to share their thoughts through a brief
feedback flow, including a comment field and NPS-style rating.

Event Flow & Elements - QR Code Scanning
Introduction
This manual provides step-by-step instructions for event organizers on how to use the Frankie
QR code registration and check-in system during Opening Week events. It explains the
process from ticket distribution to student check-in and record management.
Prerequisites
Before the event:
1. Students must apply to the event and will receive a digital ticket with a unique QR code via
WhatsApp.
2. Organizers will receive an email containing access links to the QR code scanner.
3. Each organizer or staff member who needs to scan tickets must have a separate scanner
link.
Process Overview
Step 1 – Ticket Distribution
1. Students register for the event.
2. Each student receives their digital ticket (QR code) automatically.
3. These tickets are unique and linked to each student.
Step 2 – Receiving the Scanner Link
1. Organizers receive an email within the time window defined during event setup in the
Dashboard.
2. The email contains a button to launch the QR code scanner directly in the mobile browser.
3. No app installation is required.
4. Each scanner link is unique to one user and cannot be shared.

1. Click the button in the email to open the scanner in your mobile browser.

4. The scanned ticket is immediately saved in the CRM system, updating the attendance list.

System Rules
1. Duplicate prevention: The same QR code cannot be scanned twice. This prevents a student
from being checked in multiple times or from reusing someone elseʼs ticket.
2. Real-time updates: Attendance data is instantly stored in the CRM system, which ensures
organizers always have an up-to-date list of attendees.
3. New ticket issuance: Organizers can issue tickets on the spot for students who arrive without
one, ensuring no student is turned away unnecessarily.
Troubleshooting
1. Camera not opening? Ensure your browser has camera permissions enabled.
2. Scanner link not working? Please verify you are using the unique link from your email and
that it has not expired.
3. Do multiple staff members need scanners? Use the email button to request additional links.
4. Student ticket not scanning? Verify that the QR code is clear and undamaged. If issues
persist, issue a replacement ticket using the scanner tool.
Support
If you encounter persistent issues during the event, contact the Frankie support team or your
designated event coordinator.

Frankie Knowledgebase
How does Frankie work?
When a user asks a question, Frankie analyzes it to understand what it means. Then, Frankie
searches through the knowledge base to find the paragraph containing information that best
matches the question. The most relevant paragraphs are used to generate a clear, helpful,
human-like answer.
Frankie relies entirely on the content in the knowledge base to answer questions. If something
isnʼt written there, Frankie doesnʼt know it and canʼt talk about it.

BookStack Structure & Features
Frankieʼs knowledge base is built using BookStack, an easy-to-use platform organized in a
familiar book-like format: Shelf → Book → Chapter → Page
Shelf
Highest level of organization, used to differentiate between language versions of the knowledge
base. One shelf is Frankie - English, and another Frankie - German.
Books
These are the second highest level of organization, typically based on a user group,
department, or service. For example: book Language courses contains chapters and pages
related to language courses at Frankfurt School.
Chapters
Used to group related pages within a book. Chapters canʼt store content and they donʼt have a
function other than for grouping content visually.
Pages
Pages are what we refer to as a knowledge base file. It is where the actual knowledge content
lives. Each page should focus on a specific topic or process.

Formatting & Style Rules
Use line breaks between paragraphs
To improve readability and help Frankie better understand the structure of the content, always
insert a line break (by pressing Enter) between paragraphs. In BookStack, these line breaks will
show as horizontal lines.
Paragraph length limits
BookStack will prevent you from saving a paragraph if it exceeds the allowed character count
(1,000 characters). Overlong paragraphs will turn red in the editor. To fix this, simply break the
content into shorter, clearer sections. Keep in mind to provide context in every paragraph.
Context in every paragraph
When writing content for the knowledge base, itʼs important to remember that Frankie treats
each paragraph as a separate, self-contained unit. It does not understand the context from
surrounding paragraphs, from the knowledge base file name or from any other source.
In the first version, Frankie doesnʼt know what “it” refers to because the paragraph lacks an
explicit subject. To ensure Frankie can correctly interpret and surface your content, always write
each paragraph as a clear and complete statement.
Best practice: Avoid vague references like “it,” “they,” “this,” or “that” unless you clearly identify
the subject within the same paragraph.
